# Measured and expected tire wear

A tire with usable tread measurements shows the lifespan implied by its observed wear rate next to the expected lifespan, and the gap between them. The expected lifespan is the one entered for the tire when there is one; otherwise it comes from the average tread-wear rate. Without a usable measurement, the comparison is hidden.

The observed rate is the tread lost divided by the distance driven while the tire was mounted: the raw measured rate, not the blended rate used by replacement forecasts. The lifespans assume that wear rate stays constant and are not replacement promises. Telemetry-adjusted lifespan is a separate figure.

The gap is expressed on the lifespan: negative means the tire wears out sooner than expected. A gap beyond 20% of the entered lifespan is amber when shorter and green when longer. The comparison stays neutral when the measure rests on few worn millimetres (the forecast then leans on the average rate) and when the reference is the average rate rather than an entered lifespan. The colours describe the comparison and are independent of the tire's GOOD/WARNING/CRITICAL condition. Distances follow the account's distance unit.
